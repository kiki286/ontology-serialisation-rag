11. What is the difference between OBI and IDO? GOOD
- perfect:
        The Ontology-based Interoperability (OBI) framework encompasses a broader ecosystem designed to facilitate semantic interoperability across various domains. In contrast, the Industrial Data Ontology (IDO) is a specific component within this framework, serving as an upper ontology that provides high-level concepts essential for modeling industrial data. IDO defines structured terms and relationships to support data integration and automated reasoning, while OBI governs the principles and processes for developing trusted ontologies and artifacts that adhere to IDO's standards. Essentially, OBI is the overarching framework, and IDO is the foundational ontology that enables its goals.

The OBI principles document requires the use of two annotation properties rdfs:label and skos:definitions and suggests the use of many more. Annotation properties are not used by the OWL reasoner.
- actual 
        Annotation properties in the context of the Industrial Data Ontology (IDO) are RDF properties that allow for the inclusion of non-semantic information about ontology resources. These properties support various metadata and documentation needs, such as defining human-readable labels, examples of use, and provenance details. Examples include `rdfs:label` for providing a resource's name and `skos:definition` for giving a plain text definition. They are intended to enhance understanding and context without affecting the ontology's reasoning capabilities.

        The Industrial Data Ontology (IDO) defines the class `lis:Object` as a fundamental categorization that includes five primary subclasses: \n\n1. **PhysicalObject** (lis:PhysicalObject) - tangible entities with mass and spatial occupancy, such as a compressor or a wire.\n2. **InformationObject** (lis:InformationObject) - entities that represent data and information, like database records.\n3. **Location** (lis:Location) - spatial regions, e.g., a room or site.\n4. **Organization** (lis:Organization) - groups with common goals, such as a company.\n5. **FunctionalObject** (lis:FunctionalObject) - entities with specific functions or roles.\n\nThis hierarchy helps systematically classify entities found in industrial settings, facilitating effective data modeling and integration.
